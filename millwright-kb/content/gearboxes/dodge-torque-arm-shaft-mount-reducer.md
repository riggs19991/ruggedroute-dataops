> **Numbers live in the manual.** Bushing screw torques, oil quantities per mounting position and belt tensions change with every size (TA0107 through TA12608, TXT1 through TXT9). This article gives the procedure and the things people get wrong. Attach the PDF for your size to this article or to the [Manuals category](/category/manuals) so the next class has it.

## What it is

A shaft-mount reducer hangs directly on the driven shaft (conveyor head pulley, screw conveyor, mixer). A **torque arm** (turnbuckle rod) stops the case from rotating. The motor drives the input with V-belts, usually on a **motor mount** bolted to the reducer. Because the reducer hangs on the shaft, **bushing fit and torque-arm geometry** are everything.

## Bushing systems

| System | Where used | How it grips |
|---|---|---|
| **Twin tapered bushing** (TA II, "TTB") | Most Torque-Arm II sizes | Two split tapered bushings drawn into the hollow output bore from each side with cap screws; grips the shaft on both ends of the bore |
| **Straight bore with keyway and set screws** | Older TXT | Key plus set screws; the shaft must be to tolerance and straight |
| **Tapered bushing with keyway** | Some TXT | Single taper drawn in with screws |

Bushings are ordered for the **shaft diameter**; the reducer bore is the same for a range of shaft sizes. Check the shaft is straight, clean, free of burrs, and within the tolerance on the bushing sheet (typically a light clearance fit; a shaft that is undersize will let the bushing slip and wear the bore).

## Installation sequence (twin tapered bushing type)

1. **Lockout/tagout** the driven equipment and the motor. Support the reducer with a hoist; they are heavy for their size.
2. Determine the **mounting position** (which way the output shaft points, which side is up) from the manual's position diagrams. This decides the oil quantity, which plug is the level plug, and where the breather goes.
3. Clean the driven shaft and the reducer bore. Lightly oil the **outside** taper of the bushings and the screw threads; keep the shaft-to-bushing interface dry unless the sheet says otherwise.
4. Install the **key** in the shaft keyway (full-length key supplied with the bushing kit).
5. Slide the reducer onto the shaft, with the **inner bushing** placed first as the manual shows (some kits install the inner bushing before the reducer goes on). Position the reducer where the belt drive will be.
6. Insert the **outer bushing**, then the cap screws through the bushing flanges into the tapped holes in the hub. Some sizes use a back-up plate.
7. **Tighten the cap screws alternately and evenly**, in stages, to the torque on the bushing sheet. Uneven tightening cocks the bushing and the reducer runs out of true. Do not use the removal (jack) holes for installation.
8. Check the reducer is square to the shaft and the output hub runs true (dial indicator on the hub face).
9. **Torque arm**: bolt the torque-arm bracket to the reducer case and anchor the rod to a rigid structure. The rod must be roughly **perpendicular (90°, within about ±30°) to a line from the output shaft centre to the rod anchor**, and it must be free to swing slightly with belt tension changes. It must **never** be anchored in a way that puts the reducer in bending, and it must never be rigidly bolted at both ends without the pivot bushings. Set the turnbuckle so the belts will be tensionable by moving the motor, not by pulling the reducer.
10. **Backstop** (if fitted, common on inclined conveyors): confirm the free rotation direction matches the conveyor direction **before** connecting the motor. Running a backstop backwards destroys it. Backstop reducers need the oil the backstop maker allows (usually no EP additives).
11. **Oil**: remove the shipping plug, install the breather in the top port for your mounting position, fill through the fill port to the level plug for that position, using the quantity in the manual as a guide (the level plug is the authority). Never run dry: units ship **without oil**.
12. **Belt drive**: fit the input sheave and motor sheave (bushings torqued), align, tension per the belt guide, re-check the torque-arm rod angle after tensioning.
13. **Guard** the belts and the shaft.
14. Bump the motor, check rotation, listen, check the torque-arm rod is in tension/compression and not flexing the structure.
15. After the first day, re-check bushing screw torque, belt tension, oil level, and the torque-arm anchor bolts.

## Removal

Back out the cap screws, thread them into the **jack (removal) holes** in the bushing flange, and tighten evenly to break the taper. Do not hammer.

## Common problems

| Problem | Cause |
|---|---|
| Reducer rocks on the shaft, fretting rust at the bore | Bushing screws not torqued, shaft undersize, keyless installation |
| Oil leaking from the input seal | Overfilled for the mounting position, plugged breather |
| Torque arm bracket cracked | Rod at a bad angle, anchor too rigid, shock loads |
| Hot case, oil dark | Wrong oil quantity for position, wrong grade, overload |
| Backstop failed | Wrong direction, EP oil |
