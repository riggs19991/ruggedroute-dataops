## Oil selection

| Gear type | Typical oil | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Helical, spur, bevel (enclosed) | ISO VG 220 EP (AGMA 5 EP) at normal ambient; VG 320 (AGMA 6) for hot or slow drives; VG 150 for cold | EP (extreme pressure) mineral or synthetic PAO |
| **Worm gear** | ISO VG 460 or 680 **compounded** (AGMA 7 Comp / 8 Comp) or PAG synthetic | **Do not use EP sulphur-phosphorus oils** with bronze worm wheels; they attack yellow metal. Many worm units are factory filled with synthetic |
| Shaft-mount / screw conveyor reducers | Per nameplate; often VG 220 EP | |
| Planetary (gearmotors) | Per manual; some use grease | |

Read the **nameplate**. It usually gives the ISO grade and the quantity for each mounting position. Do not mix mineral and synthetic oils, or PAG with anything else.

ISO VG number = viscosity in cSt at 40°C. AGMA number is the older US grade (AGMA 5 ≈ ISO 220).

## Oil level

![Sight glass level, breather, magnetic plug and gear dip](/img/gearboxes/oil-level-and-breather.svg)

*Sight glass level, breather, magnetic plug and gear dip*

1. The level is only valid with the reducer **stopped and cooled** for a few minutes so oil drains back.
2. **Sight glass**: level at the mark or mid-glass. **Level plug**: oil just reaches the bottom of the plug hole.
3. **Mounting position matters.** A reducer mounted on its side or vertically has different fill plugs, level plugs and quantities than the standard horizontal position. Manuals show a diagram of each position with which port is fill, level, drain and breather. Filling to the wrong plug either starves the top bearings or floods the seals.
4. Overfilling causes churning, foaming, heat and leaks at the seals. Underfilling starves the gears and the upper bearings.

## Breather

- The breather lets the case breathe as it heats and cools. A plugged breather pushes oil past the seals; a missing breather sucks in dust and water.
- Must be at the **top** in the actual mounting position. When a reducer is remounted, move the breather to the new top port and plug the old one.
- Clean or replace on the PM. Desiccant breathers for wet or dusty plants.

## Oil change intervals (typical)

| Condition | First change | Then |
|---|---|---|
| Mineral oil | after **500 hours** or 2-4 weeks (flushes break-in wear) | every **2 500 hours** or 6 months, whichever first |
| Synthetic | often no break-in change required | 5 000-10 000 hours or per manual |
| Hot (> 80°C oil) or dirty environment | halve the interval | |

Better: **oil analysis** (viscosity, water, particle count, iron/copper) every 3-6 months and change on condition. Drain hot, inspect the drain plug magnet and the drained oil for metal.

## Routine inspection

| Check | Limit / what to look for |
|---|---|
| Case temperature | Oil should typically stay under **90°C (200°F)**; a reducer too hot to hold a hand on for 5 seconds (about 60°C) is normal for many, but a sudden rise is a warning |
| Noise | Whine that changes with load = gears; grinding/rumble = bearings; knock = backlash or broken tooth |
| Leaks | Seals, split line, breather, plugs. Oil on the shaft = seal |
| Vibration | Compare to baseline; gear mesh frequency = teeth × rpm |
| Oil level and condition | Milky = water; dark and burnt smell = overheated; metallic sparkle = wear |
| Mounting bolts, torque arm, foundation | Loose bolts show as fretting rust dust |
| Cooling fan/shroud | Blocked fins run hot |

## Backlash check

Backlash is the free play between meshing teeth. Too little = binding and heat; too much = shock and noise.

1. Lock the input shaft.
2. Mount a dial indicator with the plunger tangential to the output shaft at a known radius (or on a lever clamped to the shaft).
3. Rock the output shaft gently both ways by hand. The indicator swing is the backlash **at that radius**. Convert to the gear pitch line if the manual specifies it there: backlash at pitch line = reading × (pitch radius ÷ measurement radius).
4. Compare to the manual (often 0.003-0.010" for small industrial helical units). A growing backlash over successive PMs is tooth wear.

## Seal replacement notes

- Protect the seal lip from keyway edges with tape or a sleeve when sliding it on.
- Lip faces the oil. Lubricate the lip.
- Check the shaft for a groove where the old seal ran; a speedi-sleeve fixes it without machining.
