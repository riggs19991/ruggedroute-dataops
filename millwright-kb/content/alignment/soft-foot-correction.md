## What it is

Soft foot is a machine foot that does not sit flat on the base when the bolts are loose. Tightening the bolt pulls the frame down to the base and **distorts the machine**, which moves the shaft and the bearings. Alignment readings taken on a distorted frame change every time a bolt is touched, and the motor frame is under permanent stress. Rule: **any foot that lifts more than 0.002" (0.05 mm) when its bolt is loosened must be corrected.**

## Types

![Parallel, angular, squishy and induced soft foot](/img/alignment/soft-foot-types.svg)

*Parallel, angular, squishy and induced soft foot*

| Type | Description | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Parallel (short) foot** | Foot is flat but higher than the others (short leg, missing shim) | Add shims equal to the gap |
| **Angular (bent) foot** | Foot touches on one edge; a feeler gauge shows a tapered gap | Step-shim (tapered shim pack) or machine the foot |
| **Squishy foot** | Dirt, paint, rust, burrs or a stack of bent shims between foot and base | Clean it; replace the shim stack with a few clean shims |
| **Induced soft foot** | External force, usually pipe strain or a warped base, pulls the frame | Fix the piping/base; nothing you do at the foot will hold |

## Check (dial indicator method)

1. Machine rough-aligned, all four bolts torqued.
2. Set a magnetic-base dial indicator on the base with the plunger on the top of foot #1 near the bolt.
3. Zero. Loosen bolt #1 fully. Read the lift. Re-tighten.
4. Repeat for each foot, one at a time, always re-tightening before moving to the next.
5. Any foot over 0.002" is soft. Usually one or two feet show the problem; the diagonal pattern (two opposite corners lift) is classic.

Feeler-gauge method: loosen one bolt and probe under the foot from all four sides. Equal gap all round = parallel; gap on one side only = angular.

## Correct

1. **Clean** the underside of every foot and the base. Wire brush, scrape paint, stone burrs.
2. Loosen the worst foot. Measure the gap with feelers at four points around the foot.
3. **Parallel**: insert a shim equal to the smallest gap reading. Tighten. Re-check all feet.
4. **Angular**: build a tapered pack. Example gaps: 0.010" at the outer edge, 0.000" at the inner edge. Use a 0.010" shim cut to cover the outer half, then a full 0.005" shim, and so on, so the pack fills the wedge. Alternatively mill the foot flat. Tighten, re-check.
5. Never leave more than **four or five shims** under a foot. Use one thick shim instead of many thin ones.
6. Repeat the check on all four feet until every foot is under 0.002".

## Shim rules of thumb

- Stainless steel pre-cut shims, correct bolt slot size, clean and flat. Brass and plastic shims crush.
- Do not use shims with burrs, kinks, or oil between them.
- Shim under the **whole** foot. A shim that only reaches halfway creates an angular soft foot.
- If a foot needs more than about 0.125" total, look for a base problem.

## After correcting

Only now start the alignment method. If during the alignment the readings change by more than 0.002" when you tighten bolts, you have missed a soft foot or you have pipe strain: go back.
