> **The two numbers everyone must know.** Acetylene working pressure is **never above 15 psig**; free acetylene becomes unstable above that. Never draw more than **1/7 of an acetylene cylinder's contents per hour** or you pull acetone out with the gas. Everything else in this procedure builds on those.

![Cylinders, regulators, arrestors, check valves, hoses and torch](/img/oxy-fuel/oxy-acetylene-hookup.svg)

*Cylinders, regulators, arrestors, check valves, hoses and torch*

## 1. Cylinders

![Oxygen and acetylene cylinders with regulators fitted, chained upright](/photos/oxy-fuel/cylinders-regulators.jpg)

*Oxygen and acetylene cylinders with regulators fitted, chained upright. Photo: James Halliday, CC BY-SA 2.0, via commons*

1. Cylinders stand **upright**, chained or strapped to a cart or wall. Acetylene must always be upright (or lie flat and then stand for at least an hour before use so the acetone settles).
2. Caps stay on until the cylinder is secured. Never lift a cylinder by the cap.
3. Look at the cylinder valve outlets. If dirt is present, stand to the side and **crack** the valve open and closed for an instant to blow it out ("cracking"). Do this **away from sparks, flame or people**, and never crack an acetylene cylinder near an ignition source.
4. Check the hydrostatic test date on the cylinder collar. Out-of-date cylinders go back to the supplier.

## 2. Regulators

![Two gauges on each regulator: cylinder pressure and working pressure](/photos/oxy-fuel/regulator-gauges.jpg)

*Two gauges on each regulator: cylinder pressure and working pressure. Photo: J.Dncsn, CC BY-SA 3.0, via commons*

| Gas | Thread on the cylinder nut | Hose colour | Hose fitting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oxygen | Right-hand (turns clockwise to tighten), smooth nut | Green | "B" size, right-hand |
| Acetylene | **Left-hand** (turns counter-clockwise to tighten), nut has a **groove/notch** | Red | "B" size, left-hand, grooved nut |

1. Inspect the regulator inlet filter and the cylinder valve seat for damage or **any trace of oil or grease. Oil + high-pressure oxygen = fire.** Keep greasy gloves off oxygen fittings.
2. Thread the regulator on by hand, then snug with a wrench. Do not force. A cross-threaded CGA fitting will leak.
3. **Back the adjusting screw (T-handle) all the way out** (counter-clockwise until it turns freely). A regulator left "in" will slam full cylinder pressure into the low-pressure side when the cylinder is opened and can rupture the diaphragm.

## 3. Hoses, check valves, flashback arrestors

1. Fit **reverse-flow check valves** and **flashback arrestors** at the torch inlet (or at the regulator outlet if that is how your shop is set up). Victor torches with the "FC" suffix have them built in.
2. Connect the green hose to the oxygen regulator and the red hose to the acetylene regulator. Left-hand fittings only go on left-hand fittings; if it will not thread, you have the wrong hose.
3. Inspect hoses for cuts, burns, and bulges. A hose repaired with tape is scrap.

## 4. Torch and tip

![A portable oxy-acetylene cart: cylinders chained, hoses coiled, torch on the hook](/photos/oxy-fuel/torch-cart.jpg)

*A portable oxy-acetylene cart: cylinders chained, hoses coiled, torch on the hook. Photo: Paul Anderson, CC BY 2.0, via commons*

1. Connect the torch handle to the hoses. Snug with a wrench.
2. For **welding**, screw the welding tip/mixer straight onto the handle. For **cutting**, attach the cutting attachment (CA2460 on a 315 handle, CA1350 on a 100 handle) and open the handle's **oxygen valve fully**; preheat oxygen is then controlled with the attachment's preheat valve.
3. Pick the tip from the [Victor tip chart](/article/victor-tip-chart-acetylene) for the metal thickness. Inspect the tip: clean seat, no nicks, orifices clear. Clean with tip cleaners of the correct size, pushing straight in and out, never twisting.
4. Seat the tip and tighten the tip nut with a wrench. Victor specifies **15 to 20 ft-lb**. Hand-tight is not enough; over-tightening damages the seat.

## 5. Opening the cylinders

Stand so the cylinder valve is between you and the regulator gauge face. **Never stand in front of or behind a regulator** when opening a cylinder; face the valve from the side.

1. **Oxygen**: open the cylinder valve **slowly** until it just moves the gauge, then open it **all the way**. Oxygen valves are double-seated; fully open prevents leaks around the stem.
2. **Acetylene**: open the cylinder valve slowly, **1/4 to 3/4 turn only** (never more than 1½ turns), and **leave the wrench on the valve** so it can be closed instantly.

## 6. Setting working pressures

Do this with the torch valves **open** so you are reading flowing pressure, which is what the tip chart lists.

1. Open the torch **oxygen** valve (on a cutting attachment: handle oxygen valve fully open, then press or open the cutting-oxygen lever) and turn the oxygen regulator screw **clockwise** until the low-pressure gauge shows the chart value. Close the torch valve.
2. Open the torch **acetylene** valve and set the acetylene regulator to the chart value, **never above 15 psig**. Close the valve.
3. Re-check both gauges after a few seconds. A creeping low-pressure gauge with the torch closed means the regulator seat is leaking; take it out of service.

Typical starting pressures for a hand-held Victor cutting torch on mild steel (from the 65-2505 chart):

| Metal thickness | Tip (1-101 / 3-101) | Cutting oxygen (psig) | Acetylene (psig) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1/8" | 000 | 20-25 | 3-5 |
| 1/4" | 00 | 20-25 | 3-5 |
| 3/8" | 0 | 25-30 | 3-5 |
| 1/2" | 0 | 30-35 | 3-5 |
| 3/4" | 1 | 30-35 | 3-5 |
| 1" | 2 | 35-40 | 3-6 |
| 2" | 3 | 40-45 | 4-8 |
| 3" | 4 | 40-50 | 5-11 |
| 4" | 5 | 45-55 | 6-13 |

Welding tips (W-J / 100 series) run both gases at roughly the same low pressure: 3-5 psig for tips 000 to 2, rising to about 10 psig oxygen / 8 psig acetylene at tip 7. See the full chart article for every size.

## 7. Leak test and purge

1. With cylinders open and regulators set, close both torch valves and brush **approved leak-detection solution** (not soapy water with oil content) on every joint: cylinder-to-regulator, regulator-to-hose, hose-to-torch, torch-to-attachment, tip nut. Bubbles = tighten or replace. **Never leak-check with a flame.**
2. Purge each line separately: open the oxygen torch valve for a few seconds, close; open the acetylene valve for a few seconds, close. This clears air/mixed gas from the hoses. Purge away from ignition sources.

## 8. Lighting

![Carburising, neutral and oxidising flames](/img/oxy-fuel/flame-types.svg)

![The three flames in a photo: carburizing with its feather, neutral, oxidizing](/photos/oxy-fuel/flame-types-photo.jpg)

*The three flames in a photo: carburizing with its feather, neutral, oxidizing. Photo: Lajoswinkler, CC BY-SA 4.0, via commons*

*Carburising, neutral and oxidising flames*

1. Wear #5 shade goggles for cutting/heavy welding (#3-4 for light brazing), leather gloves, long sleeves, no synthetics.
2. Open the torch **acetylene** valve about **1/8 to 1/4 turn**. Light with a **friction striker**. Never use a lighter or matches.
3. Increase acetylene until the flame stops smoking and the black soot at the flame tip disappears (the flame will just start to jump away from the tip, then settle when you back off slightly).
4. Open the torch (or preheat) **oxygen** valve slowly. The flame shortens, the feather (acetylene excess) shrinks toward the inner cone. Stop when the feather **just disappears** and you have a sharp, bright inner cone with a blue outer envelope. That is a **neutral flame**, used for almost all cutting and steel welding.
   - Feather still visible = **carburising** (excess acetylene). Used for hard-facing, some brazing.
   - Short, hissing, pointed cone = **oxidising** (excess oxygen). Burns steel; only used for some brass work.
5. For a cutting attachment: after the preheat flame is neutral, press the **cutting-oxygen lever** and re-check the preheat flame; adjust the preheat oxygen valve until it is neutral *with the lever pressed*, since the cutting jet steals a little preheat oxygen.

## 9. Making a cut (mild steel)

![Cutting torch at work: preheat flames on the plate, oxygen lever pressed](/photos/oxy-fuel/cutting-closeup.jpg)

*Cutting torch at work: preheat flames on the plate, oxygen lever pressed. Photo: Jonas Boni, CC BY 2.0, via commons*

1. Hold the tip so the preheat cones are about **1/16" to 1/8" above** the plate, torch perpendicular to the surface (tilt slightly into the direction of travel for thin plate).
2. Preheat the edge until it is bright cherry red.
3. Press the cutting-oxygen lever fully and move at the chart speed (e.g. 15-19 ipm for 1" plate with a #2 tip). Too slow = wide kerf and gouged bottom; too fast = slag sticks and the cut stalls.
4. Keep the tip clean. If the flame pops (backfire) or you hear a shrill hiss inside the torch (flashback), **close the oxygen torch valve immediately, then the fuel valve**, and let the torch cool before finding the cause (dirty tip, tip too close, loose tip nut, wrong pressures).

## 10. Shutdown - Victor order

Victor's manual (0056-3260, sections 6.05 and 7.01) is explicit:

1. **Close the torch oxygen valve first**, then the torch **fuel** (acetylene) valve. Closing oxygen first extinguishes without the pop that can push flame back into the tip.
   *(Many shops teach fuel-first to avoid a soot puff. Follow your instructor and the equipment manual; the critical part is that both are closed before the next step.)*
2. Close the **acetylene cylinder valve**, then the **oxygen cylinder valve**.
3. Open the torch **acetylene** valve to drain the acetylene hose and regulator; both acetylene gauges fall to zero. Close the torch valve.
4. Open the torch **oxygen** valve to drain the oxygen side; both oxygen gauges fall to zero. Close the torch valve.
5. Back out both regulator adjusting screws until they turn freely.
6. Hang the torch up, tip pointing down. Coil hoses. Cap cylinders if they are being moved.

Any gauge that will not drop to zero means a cylinder valve is still cracked or a regulator is leaking. Fix it before you walk away.

## Troubleshooting quick list

| Symptom | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Flame pops out on lighting | Acetylene too low, tip partly blocked, or oxygen opened first |
| Gauge creeps up with torch closed | Regulator seat worn, take out of service |
| Cannot get neutral flame with feather gone | Acetylene set too high for the tip, or tip too large for the pressures |
| Cut stalls partway | Travel too fast, preheat too low, cutting oxygen pressure too low, dirty tip |
| Cutting oxygen lever hisses but no cut | Cutting oxygen channel blocked; clean or replace tip |
| Squeal/whistle in torch | Flashback; shut down immediately (oxygen then fuel), cool, inspect |
