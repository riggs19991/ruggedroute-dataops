## Gas facts you must know

| | Oxygen | Acetylene |
|---|---|---|
| Cylinder pressure (full) | about 2 200 psig | about 250 psig (dissolved in acetone in a porous filler) |
| Working pressure limit | as per chart (up to ~60 psig cutting) | **15 psig maximum** |
| Hazard | Not flammable itself but makes everything burn violently; **oil and grease ignite spontaneously** in high-pressure oxygen | Unstable above 15 psig; flammable in air from 2.5% to 81% |
| Withdrawal limit | - | **1/7 of cylinder contents per hour** (pulls acetone if exceeded) |
| Cylinder valve | Open fully (double-seated) | Open 1/4 to 3/4 turn, wrench left on valve |
| Fitting thread | Right-hand | Left-hand, notched nut |

## Storage and transport

- Store oxygen and fuel-gas cylinders **at least 20 ft apart**, or separated by a 5 ft, half-hour fire-rated barrier (OSHA 1910.253).
- Upright, chained, capped, in a ventilated area away from heat, electrical panels and traffic. Acetylene never on its side in storage.
- Move cylinders on a cart. Never roll acetylene cylinders on their edge, never drag them with a chain sling, never lift by the cap.
- Empty cylinders are marked "MT" or tagged and stored separately with valves closed and caps on.

## Regulators and fittings

- Back out the adjusting screw before opening a cylinder. Stand to the side of the gauges.
- Never use oil, grease, PTFE tape or pipe dope on any oxygen fitting. CGA seats are metal-to-metal.
- A regulator that creeps (low-pressure gauge climbs with the torch closed) has a failed seat. Tag it out.
- Use only leak-detection fluid approved for oxygen service.

## Check valves and flashback arrestors

- **Reverse-flow check valve**: stops one gas backing up the other hose (higher-pressure oxygen into the acetylene line, for example). Fit at the torch inlets.
- **Flashback arrestor**: quenches a flame front travelling back up the hose. Fit at the torch and/or regulator. Replace after any flashback.

## Backfire vs flashback

| Event | What you hear/see | Cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Backfire** | Loud pop, flame goes out or relights at the tip | Tip touched the work, tip overheated, dirty tip, pressures too low | Close oxygen torch valve then fuel valve, check tip and pressures, relight |
| **Sustained backfire** | Flame burns *inside* the tip with a snapping sound | Same causes, worse | Shut down immediately, oxygen first |
| **Flashback** | Shrill whistle/hiss, black smoke, flame burning back in the torch or hose | Mixed gases in the hose (no purge), wrong pressures, leaking mixer | **Close oxygen torch valve, then fuel valve**, then cylinder valves. Let cool. Inspect torch, replace arrestors, find the cause before relighting |

## Personal protection

- Shade **#5** goggles for cutting; #3-4 for light brazing/soldering. Face shield over goggles for overhead work.
- Leather gloves, leather or FR cotton clothing, no cuffs, no synthetics, boots. Long sleeves buttoned.
- Hearing protection near cutting.
- Ventilation: cutting galvanised or painted steel produces zinc and lead fumes. Use local exhaust or a respirator.

## Hot-work controls

- A **hot-work permit** is required outside a designated welding area. Check for flammables within 35 ft, cover drains and openings, wet down or shield combustibles.
- Post a **fire watch** during the work and for at least **30 minutes** afterward (60 in many plants). Extinguisher within reach, charged and inspected.
- Confirm the item you are cutting is empty, cleaned, and not under pressure. Never cut a closed vessel, drum or tank without proper purge and gas testing.

## After the job

- Full shutdown sequence (see the setup procedure). Gauges at zero, regulators backed out.
- Check the area again for smouldering material before leaving.
- Report any regulator, hose or torch fault. Do not put a damaged torch back on the rack.
