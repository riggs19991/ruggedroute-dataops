## When to heat

Any bearing with a bore of about **50 mm (2") or more** with an interference fit on the shaft. Below that, a fitting tool and a dead-blow hammer are fine (cold mounting). The goal is to expand the inner ring enough to slide over the shaft without hammering force through the rolling elements.

## Temperature limits

| Bearing | Maximum |
|---|---|
| Standard open bearings (through-hardened steel) | **120°C (250°F)** |
| Sealed / shielded bearings with grease (2RS, 2Z) | **80°C (175°F)**; grease and seals degrade above this |
| Bearings with polyamide (nylon) cages | 120°C but heat evenly and briefly |
| Practical target for most shaft fits | 80-90°C **above ambient**, roughly 110°C (230°F) |

A 100 mm bore expands about 0.001 mm per °C, so 80°C of rise gives about 0.08 mm (0.003"), which is plenty for a typical k5/m5 fit.

## Heat sources

| Source | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Induction heater** | Preferred. Fast, clean, temperature-controlled with a probe. **Demagnetize** after heating (most units do it automatically when the cycle ends; if yours does not, run the demag cycle). A magnetized bearing collects steel wear particles. |
| Hot plate / oven | Fine for small bearings. Use a thermostat; put the bearing on a rack, not directly on the plate, and turn it over halfway. |
| Oil bath | Old method. Clean oil, bearing on a screen above the bottom (sludge). Messy; the oil must be compatible with the grease if sealed. |
| **Open flame / torch** | **Never.** Local overheating tempers the steel and distorts the ring. |

## Procedure - induction heater, shaft fit

1. Confirm the bearing number matches the drawing. Leave it in its wrapping until you are ready.
2. Measure the shaft seat with a micrometer at two places, 90° apart. Compare to the fit table (typical rotating-inner-ring fits: **j5/k5** light, **m5/m6** for heavier loads; the shaft is slightly larger than the bore). Check the shoulder is square and the fillet radius is smaller than the bearing chamfer.
3. Clean the shaft. Light oil on the seat.
4. Set the heater's probe on the inner ring. Set temperature to **110°C** (or per the bearing maker), never above 120°C. Use the largest yoke that passes through the bore.
5. Heat. When the probe reads the target, remove the bearing with heat-resistant gloves (it is hotter than it looks).
6. **Immediately** slide the bearing onto the shaft, marked/numbered face outward if the maker says so, and push it firmly against the shoulder. Hold it there with steady pressure until it grips (30-60 seconds). As it cools it will try to shrink *away* from the shoulder; if you let go too early you end up with a gap.
7. Let it cool naturally. Do not quench.
8. Fit lock nut / retaining ring, lubricate per spec, and check the shaft turns freely.

## Cold mounting (small bearings)

1. Fitting tool (impact ring/sleeve) that bears **only on the ring being pressed**: inner ring for shaft fits, outer ring for housing fits. Never transmit force through the rolling elements: that brinells the raceways and the bearing is dead before it turns.
2. Light oil on the seat. Start the bearing square; a few light taps with a dead-blow, then steady taps until it seats.
3. For both-ring interference (rare, e.g. some gearbox positions) use a tool that contacts both rings at once, or press.

## Housing fits

Bearings with a rotating **outer** ring or a stationary outer ring under heavy load may need an interference housing fit (N7/P7). Heat the housing or cool the bearing (dry ice or a freezer, wrapped, then dried quickly to avoid condensation). Loose housing fits (H7) are normal for the free bearing in a two-bearing shaft so it can float axially.

## Checks after mounting

- Bearing sits square against the shoulder (feeler gauge all round shows no gap).
- Shaft turns smoothly with no rough spots.
- For sealed bearings, seals not pushed in or damaged.
- Correct grease and amount (for open bearings about 30-50% of the free space; overpacking causes churning and heat).
- Record bearing number, date and who did it.

## Mistakes that kill new bearings

- Hammering on the outer ring to seat an inner ring (or vice versa).
- Heating a sealed bearing to 120°C.
- Unwrapping the bearing early and setting it on a dirty bench.
- Forgetting to demagnetize.
- Letting go before the ring has gripped, leaving an axial gap.
- Overfilling with grease.
